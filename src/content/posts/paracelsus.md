# Paracelsus

> *"All things are poison, and nothing is without poison; only the dose makes a thing not a poison."*
> — Paracelsus, *Septem Defensiones* (1538)

Theophrastus von Hohenheim (1493–1541), who called himself Paracelsus, spent his life picking fights with authority. He treated the ancient textbooks as opinions to be tested, not scripture. He lectured in the language his patients spoke instead of Latin, and learned as much from miners and barber-surgeons as from the universities. Most of his contemporaries thought him insufferable. Medicine has been quoting him ever since.

[The film](https://youtu.be/h9oZ1O75iZA) tells that life as a single unbroken zoom. Each chapter sits inside a round form from the chapter before: a ripple, a crucible, a wheel, a fire, a flask, a lamp, a bandage. The camera dives through each one into the next, until everything closes into a single drop. It loops, so the drop that ends the film is the drop that starts it.

## The journey

**0:00 · ROOT · Einsiedeln, 1493.** He is born beside the Devil's Bridge over the Sihl, in the Swiss canton of Schwyz. His father, a physician, teaches him plants before letters.

**0:03 · DIG · Villach, 1502.** After his mother's death the family moves to Carinthia, into mining country. The boy learns ores, metals, fire and smelting. It is the chemistry he will later carry into medicine.

**0:06 · WANDER · Across Europe, 1517.** He claims a doctorate from Ferrara and then takes to the road for years. He works as an army surgeon and learns from barbers, miners, midwives and folk healers, anyone who actually treated the sick.

**0:09 · BURN · Basel, 1527.** As city physician and professor he lectures in German and wears an alchemist's leather apron instead of academic robes. On St. John's Day he throws Avicenna's *Canon*, the standard medical text of the age, into the students' bonfire. Within a year he has to flee the city.

**0:12 · DISTILL · St. Gallen, 1531.** In the *Opus Paramirum* disease stops being an imbalance of humours and becomes chemistry: salt, sulphur and mercury. Alchemy is no longer for making gold. It is for making medicines.

**0:15 · BREATHE · Schwaz, 1533.** Among the silver miners and smelters of Tyrol he studies the lung disease that kills them. The result is the first book ever devoted to the diseases of a single occupation.

**0:18 · HEAL · Augsburg, 1536.** *Die große Wundarznei*, the Great Surgery Book, becomes his one great success in print. Its message is radical in its plainness: keep the wound clean and let nature do the healing.

**0:21 · WEIGH · Carinthia, 1538.** Defending himself against critics who called his mineral remedies poisons, he writes the sentence above. Whether a substance cures or kills depends on the amount. It is the founding idea of toxicology.

**0:24 · PARACELSUS · Salzburg, 1541.** He dies at forty-seven, in an inn in Salzburg. His ideas outlived the books he burned.

## What he left behind

- **Toxicology.** Harm is a matter of dose, not of the substance itself.
- **Chemistry in the service of medicine.** He turned the alchemist's laboratory toward the pharmacy, against the humoral theory of Galen.
- **Occupational medicine.** He showed that the work people do can be what makes them ill.
- **Gentler surgery.** Clean wounds, minimal interference, nature as the healer.
- **Mental illness as illness.** He treated madness as a disease of the body rather than a possession of the soul.
- **A word.** He gave the metal zinc its name.

He was also a mystic, an astrologer and a writer of strange cosmologies. Like most turning points in the history of science, he stood with one foot in the old world. The foot he planted in the new one has not moved.

## How it was made

The film was produced end to end by Claude (Anthropic): the concept, the prompt, the compositing, the camera, the typography and the original score, all written as code. The eight scene paintings were generated with ChatGPT Images, upscaled with Real-ESRGAN and given depth maps so near objects move faster than far ones as the camera pushes in. Everything else is a Python compositor. It handles the nested zoom, the black, bone and sulphur-yellow palette with its engraving-line texture, the headlines the camera flies through, and the rolling year counter. The music is synthesized from scratch at 120 BPM: a glass-harmonica motif in D minor that lifts to D major for the title.

It is 28 seconds at 1080p and 60 frames per second: one shot, no cuts, and a seamless loop.
