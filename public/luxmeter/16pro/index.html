EyeLux · Phase 0 · iPhone 16 Pro

Camera test on your iPhone 16 Pro

About 45 minutes in two sittings: 15 in daylight, 30 after dark. Only the iPhone, no special equipment.

Thank you for helping. EyeLux is an app that will measure how much light reaches your eyes, in lux, using only the iPhone's camera. Before it can do that, I need to know how the cameras behave when the app reads their raw sensor data. That's what this test checks. It doesn't measure lux yet.

Why your iPhone: these checks have already run on an iPhone 14 Pro and a 17 Pro. A 16 Pro shows whether what I've learned holds across models.

Two sittings. Part 1 is in daylight, indoors by a window. Part 2 needs real darkness, so start it at least an hour after sunset: a curtained room in the daytime lets in more light than you'd think.

Tip: keep this page open on an iPad or a computer while you use the iPhone, and tick the boxes as you go (the ticks are remembered on the device you're reading on). If you only have the iPhone, read each check through before you start it.

Before you start

Install the app

What you need

  • One sheet of plain white printer paper
  • A dark cloth (a sock or a towel will do)
  • A tape measure, at least 2 m long
  • A lamp with an ordinary bulb you can see (take the shade off if it comes off easily), not on a dimmer
  • A few books to lean the iPhone against
  • The iPhone charged to at least half

Good to know
  • The screen goes black while measuring. That's on purpose: in the dark checks the screen's own light would spoil the result. Wait until it comes back. That takes a few seconds, up to about 15 with a bright lamp in view, and about a minute for a sweep.
  • Don't press the Camera Control, the flat button low on the right-hand edge. It opens Apple's Camera app. If that happens, go back to EyeLux and repeat the step.
  • Never tap Clear. It deletes all your results.
  • Leave "Black screen while measuring" on. Leave "Save DNG files" off, except in check C.

Part 1 · Daylight

ACamera details Any time · 2 min

The basic facts about each camera: which raw format it offers, its shortest and longest shutter times, and its sensitivity range.

If the status line says no Bayer RAW format!, take the screenshot anyway. That's a result too.

BSteady response Daylight · 5 min

Checks that the camera's numbers stay in proportion to the light when the app changes shutter speed and sensitivity, including the very high sensitivity it uses in the dark. A sweep takes about 15 pictures of the same scene at different settings.

Best when the daylight is steady, not with clouds racing past the sun. Each sweep ends with a summary in bold; no need to check the numbers.

CCorners vs centre Daylight · 5 min

Shows how much darker the picture gets towards its corners. That decides how the app weighs light arriving from different directions.

This saves two photo files of about 24 MB each. You'll send them at the end.

Part 2 · After dark, at least an hour after sunset

DVery dark, both cameras After dark · 10 min

How little light the camera can still tell apart from none at all. The light levels that matter here are those in a bedroom at night, far below what you could read by.

Keep the iPad or computer screen face down while capturing: in a dark room it's a light source too. If a covered shot says BURNT OUT, light got in: cover it more firmly and capture again. If a Manual dim shot says BURNT OUT, the room is brighter than needed: let in less light and capture it again.

EOne lamp, two distances After dark · 15 min

Light from a small lamp gets four times weaker when you double the distance. This checks that the camera sees exactly that, with the bright bulb itself in the picture. To see both the bulb and the dark room around it, the app takes two or three pictures at different exposures and combines them (HDR), so the screen stays black for up to about 15 seconds.

Each result line should mention HDR. If one says BURNT OUT, capture again once; if it still does, carry on.

Send me the results

If something goes wrong

  • An error in the status line: take a screenshot and send it, and carry on with the next check if you can.
  • The app stops responding, or the screen stays black for more than two minutes: close it (swipe up from the bottom and flick it away) and open it again. Captures you've already made are kept.
  • Apple's Camera app opened: the Camera Control was pressed. Go back to EyeLux and repeat the step.
  • You captured something by mistake: no problem. Type a note on the next capture, for example redo, and repeat the step.
  • Don't tap Clear. It deletes all saved results and photo files.
