Road book · 10–12 November 2026 · non-stop, one driver

Siracusa→Riga

The extreme version, with no hotels. One driver spends about 39 hours at the wheel over 54 hours, in stints of about two hours. Every four hours there’s a 20-minute nap in the car, and each night you sleep three hours at a lit, 24-hour motorway stop. Leave Tuesday at 04:00 and you’re home on Thursday at about 10:45 Riga time.

The rules that make it work

At 56, driving alone, the risk isn’t the distance. It’s a few seconds of microsleep at 130 km/h. Each rule is there to prevent that.

1 · Two hours, then stop

  • About 2 hours at the wheel, never more than 2 h 20. That stretch happens twice, where the next good stop is further on.
  • At a 15-minute stop, get out of the car, walk, use the toilet and drink water.

2 · A 20-minute nap every four hours

  • Drink a coffee first, then recline the seat and set an alarm for 20 minutes. The caffeine takes effect just as you wake up.
  • Don’t sleep longer than 25 minutes, or you wake up groggy. Take the nap even if you feel fine, especially the 14:00 one on Tuesday.

3 · Sleep through the body’s low point

  • Each night, sleep 3 hours: two 90-minute sleep cycles. Waking at the end of a cycle is easier than waking in the middle of one.
  • After the alarm, spend 15 minutes outside: cold water on your face, light, and coffee. Then drive.
  • No coffee after 22:00. At the night stop you need to fall asleep straight away.

4 · Stop at once if…

  • …you yawn again and again, your eyelids are heavy or your head nods, you drift onto the rumble strip, miss an exit, or can’t remember the last few kilometres.
  • Then take the next parking area and a 20-minute nap. If two naps don’t fix it, sleep a full 90 minutes, which costs 1½ hours of arrival time. Open windows and loud music don’t help.

Set up Waze once

  • Settings → Navigation: allow toll roads and ferries, and don’t avoid highways.
  • Settings → Navigation → Toll passes / Vignettes: add the Austria and Czech Republic vignettes, or Waze may send you off the motorway there.
  • Settings → Gas stations: set the preferred fuel to LPG.

Set up Google Maps once

  • On the route screen, tap ⋯ → Route options and make sure tolls, highways and ferries aren’t avoided.
  • Download offline maps for Italy, Austria, Czechia, Poland and the Baltics, in case you lose signal.
  • Send this file to your phone by AirDrop, email or Telegram and open it there. The buttons open the apps.

Stage 1 I · the long Italian day

Siracusa → Messina → Rome → Bologna → Gonars

1,448 km + ferry≈16½ h at the wheelsunrise 06:33sunset 16:54

The whole length of Italy in one go. You cross the Strait at first light, take the toll-free A2 through Calabria in daylight, and pass Naples and Rome in the afternoon. In the dark you continue up the A1 past Florence and over the Apennines to Bologna, then on the A13 and A4 to Friuli. You sleep at Gonars Sud, an hour short of the Austrian border.

Fuel and tolls

  • LPG on your side at Sala Consilina, Mascherone, Po Est and Gonars, about every 400 km.
  • Keep a quarter tank of petrol for the ferry.
  • Tolls: the A18, then the A30, A1, A13, A4 and A23, ≈€75–85 by card. The A2 is free.

Night roadworks

  • Italian motorways often close sections overnight for roadworks, usually from about 22:00, with signed detours.
  • On Tuesday afternoon, check the closures for the A13 on autostrade.it and for the A4 east of Venice on autostradealtoadriatico.it.

Heads-up

  • Around Naples and Caserta, lock the car and take your valuables at every stop.
  • Speed limit 130, 110 in rain. Average-speed cameras (Tutor) cover many stretches.

Fuel and tolls

  • Austria has almost no LPG on this route. The car switches to petrol somewhere past Graz, and the next LPG is right after the Czech border.
  • Austria: digital vignette, the 10-day one for €12.80 from Tue 10 (or the 1-day for €9.60 for Wed 11 only). Czechia: 1-day e-vignette for Wed 11, CZK 230.
  • Poland (A1, A2, S8, S61) and Lithuania are free for cars.

Winter rules today

  • A CZ Winter tyres are required in snow or ice from 1 November. Your 3PMSF tyres count if the tread is at least 4 mm.
  • LT Winter tyres are compulsory from 10 November. All-season tyres with 3PMSF are accepted, with at least 3 mm of tread.

Heads-up

  • Around Graz the A2 has air-quality 100 km/h zones (signed “IG-L”).
  • Speed limits: Poland 140 on motorways, 120 on expressways; Lithuanian motorways 110 in winter.
  • Polish MOP rest areas without a fuel station have toilets only.

Sleeping in the car

Where

  • Park in the light, near the building and its cameras, among cars and facing out.
  • Not in the truck rows: refrigerated trailers run all night and trucks leave at 04:00.
  • Resting in your car at motorway services is normal everywhere on this route. Just don’t set up camp.

Warm, with the engine off

  • Italy fines idling while parked (≈€220–440), and an idling engine is also a carbon-monoxide risk.
  • Expect about 0–5 °C at night in Friuli and Lithuania. Bring a sleeping bag, a wool hat and socks. Open a window 1 cm against condensation.

Comfortable

  • Recline the passenger seat fully; it has more room than the driver’s seat, with no steering wheel in the way.
  • Bring an eye mask, earplugs and a neck pillow, and set two alarms.

Safe

  • Lock the doors, keep the keys in your pocket and the valuables out of sight, and keep your phone charging.
  • Share your live location with someone at home for the whole trip.

If it isn’t working

Stopping for a real bed isn’t a failure. Two hotels from the fast plan are right on this route: Motel55 at Villach-Warmbad, which has self check-in and is 1½ hours past Gonars, and HOTEL AVIA Ekotrans at Piotrków, 3 minutes off the A1. Either can be booked from the car on the day. A full night’s sleep instead of a 3-hour one moves arrival from Thursday morning to Thursday afternoon or evening. That’s still a day ahead of the 4-day plan.
