Road book · 10–13 November 2026 · the way home

Siracusa→Riga

Four long days home from Sicily, with no sightseeing. Italy is behind you by Wednesday evening, Austria and Czechia take Thursday, and the Via Baltica brings you home on Friday. Every hotel lets you check out before dawn, so you can leave at first light and have breakfast in the room.

Set up Waze once

  • Settings → Navigation: allow toll roads and ferries, and don’t avoid highways.
  • Settings → Navigation → Toll passes / Vignettes: add the Austria and Czech Republic vignettes, or Waze may send you off the motorway there.
  • Settings → Gas stations: set the preferred fuel to LPG.

Set up Google Maps once

  • On the route screen, tap ⋯ → Route options and make sure tolls, highways and ferries aren’t avoided.
  • Download offline maps for Italy, Austria, Czechia, Poland and the Baltics, in case you lose signal.
  • Send this file to your phone by AirDrop, email or Telegram and open it there. The buttons open the apps.

Stage by stage

Times assume OpenStreetMap routing plus 10% for traffic and roadworks, a 15-minute break about every two hours and a 30-minute lunch. Prices are Booking.com rates for two adults in one room, without breakfast. Ratings are guest review scores.

Tue10Nov

Stage 1 I · Sicily and the south

Siracusa → Messina → Frosinone

776 km + ferry≈9¼ h at the wheelsunrise 06:32 Siracusasunset 16:52 Frosinone

Up the coast past Catania and across the Strait at Messina. Then the whole toll-free A2 through Calabria to Salerno, and the A30 and A1 north to Frosinone, an hour short of Rome. You arrive in the last of the twilight.

  1. 06:00
    Leave Siracusa with both tanks full. A18 north past Catania, then along the coast to Messina.Dawn is 06:04 and sunrise 06:32, so it’s light before Catania.
  2. 08:05
    Messina. Follow the Caronte & Tourist signs and buy a ticket at the booth (car ≈€46, no booking). Boats leave at least every 40 minutes and take about 20 to cross. Tell the crew you have LPG and drive on using petrol.Cars board at Rada San Francesco: leave the A18 at Messina Boccetta, go straight to the waterfront and follow the Caronte & Tourist signs. Boats leave Messina at 08:00, 08:40 and 09:20; arriving at about 08:05 you’ll make the 08:40 and be off in Villa by about 09:10. Leave Siracusa at 05:50 to catch the 08:00.
  3. 09:05
    Villa San Giovanni. North on the A2, toll-free to Salerno.
  4. 10:25
    Coffee and a GPL top-up at an A2 service area around Lamezia.
  5. 12:30
    Lunch around Campotenese, just past Castrovillari. The A2 climbs to about 1,000 m here.
  6. 14:50
    Short break near Salerno, then the A30 to Caserta and the A1 north.
  7. 17:15
    Frosinone. Leave the A1 at the Frosinone exit; the hotel is about 5 minutes away. Sunset is 16:52 and the last light goes at 17:21.

Sleep · Frosinone

  • Astor Hotel Vintage, Via Cicerone 194. Check-out from 01:00 Available: €78 for a double or twin. 24-hour front desk, check-in 16:30–00:00. Free private parking, including a garage, so ask for an outdoor space for the LPG car. 7.8/10 from 616 reviews.
  • Backup: Hotel degli Amici, Via Latina km 2.27, Artena, by the Valmontone exit, 40 km further. €94, check-out from 06:00, restaurant, free parking, 8.3/10 from 66 reviews. You’d arrive around 17:45.

Evening

  • The Astor has two restaurants on site. Ask about evening hours when you check in.
  • Buy Wednesday’s breakfast before you check in; you leave at 06:15.

Fuel

  • Leave Siracusa with both tanks full.
  • Top up on the A2: eight of its service areas sell GPL.
  • South of Rome the A1 has a GPL gap of about 100 km, so fill up in Frosinone this evening. Italian GPL pumps are attended, so go before they close for the night.

Heads-up

  • Tolls: about €15–20 today (A18 Catania–Messina, A30, A1). The A2 is free.
  • Board the ferry with at least a quarter tank of petrol, because LPG must be off on board.
  • A long ferry queue or A2 roadworks can push the arrival past dark. The last stretch is the A1, an easy motorway.
  • Italy: 130 km/h, 110 in rain, with average-speed “Tutor” cameras.
Wed11Nov

Stage 2 IA · over the Alps

Frosinone → Bologna → Tarvisio → Villach

825 km≈9 h at the wheelsunrise 06:50sunset 16:36 Villach

All of mainland Italy in one day, in daylight. The A1 goes around Rome on its own bypass and crosses the Apennines on the Variante di Valico. At Bologna you turn northeast for Padova and the A4. You cross the Alps at Tarvisio in the afternoon and stop on the edge of Villach before sunset.

  1. 06:15
    Leave on the A1 north. Between San Cesareo and Fiano Romano the A1 bypasses Rome, so you never touch the city’s ring road.
  2. 08:15
    Coffee near Orvieto.
  3. 11:20
    Lunch and a GPL fill at a service area as you reach Bologna. Then the A13 north to Padova and the A4 east. The Mestre bypass keeps you clear of Venice.
  4. 14:05
    Coffee on the A4 near Portogruaro.
  5. 14:45
    A23 north at Palmanova, past Udine and up the Canal Valley.
  6. 16:05
    Into Austria at Tarvisio. The Austrian vignette applies from here. Top up LPG at BP Arnoldstein, right after the border (06:00–22:00).
  7. 16:25
    Villach-Warmbad. About 7 minutes off the motorway. Sunset is 16:36.

Sleep · Villach-Warmbad

  • Motel55, Warmbader Allee 55. Check-out from 06:00 Available: €93 for a double. Self check-in until 00:00, so arrival time doesn’t matter. Free private parking, 8.8/10 from 808 reviews.
  • Backup: Pension am Campus, Bäckerteichstraße 6, Velden, 25 km further east on the A2. From €77, check-out from 06:00, but check-in only until 22:00. Free parking, 8.6/10 from 177 reviews.

Evening

  • Motel55 has no restaurant. Buy dinner and Thursday’s breakfast on the way in. Austrian supermarkets usually close at 19:00–20:00 on weekdays.

Fuel

  • Fill at lunch near Bologna. The A13 and A4 have plenty of GPL.
  • Top up at BP Arnoldstein so you enter Austria full. Austrian LPG is scarce and dear.

Heads-up

  • Tolls: about €60–65 (A1, A13, A4, A23).
  • Austrian vignette: the 10-day digital one (€12.80) covers Wednesday and Thursday. It’s valid immediately when bought online from ASFINAG.
  • Italy’s winter-tyre rules start on 15 November, after you’ve left. Austria requires winter tyres only in wintry conditions, so check the Tarvisio forecast on Tuesday evening.

Stage 3 ACZPL

Villach → Vienna → Ostrava → Piotrków

887 km≈10½ h at the wheelsunrise 07:02sunset 15:57 Piotrków

The longest day, the way you came south but in reverse. The A2 runs east over the Pack and past Graz, around Vienna, then the D52 and D1 through Czechia into Poland at Gorzyczki. The last two hours are after dark on the Polish A1, a flat, easy motorway.

  1. 06:15
    Leave on the A2 east past Klagenfurt.Dawn is 06:29, so you’re over the Pack (about 1,060 m) in daylight around 07:45.
  2. 09:05
    Coffee and an LPG top-up at PRORast Ilz, 400 m from A2 exits 138/139, open 24/7.
  3. 11:05
    Past Vienna on the A23 → S2 → S1 ring motorways, then the A5 north to the Czech border.
  4. 12:10
    Mikulov. Lunch and a full LPG fill. The Czech vignette applies from here.
  5. 13:20
    Past Brno, then the D1 northeast via Přerov to Ostrava.
  6. 15:15
    Into Poland at Gorzyczki. Coffee and cheap Polish LPG at the first station. The sun sets at about 16:05.
  7. 17:45
    Piotrków. The hotel is 2 minutes from the A1/S8 junction.

Sleep · Piotrków

  • HOTEL AVIA Ekotrans, Al. Sikorskiego 52. Check-out from 00:00 Available: €50 for a twin. Check-in until 00:00. Restaurant on site and free parking, 9.2/10 from 120 reviews.
  • Backup: Hotel Fox, Studzianki 29A, Wolbórz, right on the S8 20 km on. €52–57, restaurant, 9.1/10 from 1,795 reviews. Check-out only from 06:00 and check-in until 22:00, so you’d reach Warsaw around 07:15.

Evening

  • AVIA has a restaurant on site.
  • Buy Friday’s breakfast; you leave at 05:15. Polish supermarkets stay open late on weekdays.

Fuel

  • At Ilz, take only what you need, because Austrian LPG costs €1.22–1.60 a litre.
  • Fill fully in Mikulov (≈€0.88), then again in Poland (≈€0.69).
  • Fill up in Piotrków tonight for Friday.

Heads-up

  • Czech e-vignette: the 1-day one (CZK 230) runs until midnight. Buy it only at edalnice.gov.cz.
  • Czechia requires winter tyres from 1 November whenever there’s snow or ice, or it’s forecast.
  • Austria sometimes runs police checks near the Czech border, so have passports handy.
  • The shorter route via the B317 and S36 saves about 13 minutes but is two-lane over the Perchauer Sattel. The A2 is motorway all the way.

Stage 4 PLLTLV · home

Piotrków → Warsaw → Kaunas → Riga

825 km≈10½ h at the wheelsunrise 06:55 Piotrkówsunset 16:15 Riga

Leave before dawn so Warsaw is behind you before the morning rush. Then the S61 Via Baltica through Łomża and Suwałki into Lithuania, and the E67 home via Kaunas, Panevėžys and Bauska. Times are Polish until the border, then Baltic, one hour ahead.

  1. 05:15
    Leave AVIA and head north to Warsaw on the expressways.
  2. 06:50
    Through Warsaw on the S8, ahead of the 07:00–09:00 rush. Leave later and you should expect another 30–45 minutes.
  3. 08:00
    Coffee near Ostrów Mazowiecka, then the S61.
  4. 10:25
    Suwałki. Fill up with Polish LPG and take a break.
  5. 11:05
    Into Lithuania at Budzisko–Kalvarija. Set clocks forward one hour: it’s 12:05 in Lithuania. Times from here on are Baltic.
  6. 13:05
    Lunch in Kaunas.
  7. 15:15
    Coffee near Panevėžys.
  8. 16:30
    Into Latvia at Saločiai–Grenctāle. Sunset in Riga is 16:15.
  9. 18:00
    Riga.

Morning

  • Breakfast in the room, or coffee at the first service area on the expressway; the big fuel stations there are open around the clock.

Fuel

  • Leave full from Piotrków’s Thursday-evening fill.
  • Refill at Suwałki, about 440 km on. That leaves about 385 km to Riga.

Heads-up

  • Winter tyres are compulsory in Lithuania from 10 November, marked M+S or with the snowflake symbol. Latvia’s rule starts on 1 December.
  • Lithuania’s motorway limit is 110 km/h from November to March.
  • After dark between Panevėžys and Riga, watch for deer and elk. The road is single-carriageway in places.

Petrol as backup

  • A short run on petrol beats a long detour to find LPG, especially in Austria.
  • Keep at least a quarter tank of petrol for the Messina ferry.

Parking

  • Italy: cars with an ECE R67-01 LPG system may park above ground and on the first underground level only. The Astor has a garage, so ask for an outdoor space.
  • Tell every hotel the car runs on LPG.

Rules of the road

Winter tyres

  • LT compulsory from 10 November: M+S or snowflake marking. You cross on Friday the 13th.
  • A CZ from 1 November, whenever there’s snow, slush or ice.
  • I from 15 November, after you’ve left. LV from 1 December.

Speed limits (motorway)

  • I 130, 110 in rain, with “Tutor” average-speed cameras.
  • A 130 · CZ 130
  • PL 140, expressways 120 · LT 110 from November

In the car

  • Triangle, first-aid kit, a vest for each person, and a fire extinguisher (Lithuania and Poland require one).
  • Dipped headlights on in the daytime in Italy, Czechia, Poland and Lithuania.
  • Carry passports or ID cards for random police checks near borders.

Clocks

  • Italy, Austria, Czechia and Poland are on Central European time. Lithuania and Latvia are one hour ahead.
  • Czechia has zero alcohol tolerance. Elsewhere, assume zero for the driver.
